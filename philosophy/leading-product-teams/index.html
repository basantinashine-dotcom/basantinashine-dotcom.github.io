My Philosophy · Leadership

How I lead product teams.

Seven habits I keep, from the first conversation to launch day. Tap any picture to play it again.

  1. 1

    Invite people who see it differently

    Bring in other functions, other backgrounds and the people closest to customers before the direction is set. Different views catch what one view misses.

  2. 2

    Agree on what good looks like

    Before anyone builds, decide how we’ll know it works: the outcome, the measure and the bar. It keeps everyone pulling in the same direction.

  3. 3

    Find the failure modes first

    Ask how this could fail before it ships. A pre-mortem on day one costs far less than a post-mortem after launch.

  4. 4

    Prototype before you polish

    A scrappy prototype in front of real people answers questions a document can’t. Polish only what survives.

  5. 5

    Make progress and blockers visible

    Keep a steady rhythm for updates, blockers and the decisions that need an owner, so problems surface early and get resolved in the right room.

  6. 6

    Write down decisions and why

    Record what we decided and the reasoning behind it, so the team doesn’t relitigate settled questions or quietly reverse them.

  7. 7

    Praise in public, coach in private

    Call out good work where the whole team can see it. Give hard feedback one to one, with care.
